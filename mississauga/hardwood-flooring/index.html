Planning the Installation

Hardwood Flooring Planning in Mississauga

A flooring plan starts with how each room is used, which surfaces are being replaced, and the condition of the existing floor. Material selection, plank width, finish, installation layout, and preparation can then be discussed for the agreed scope.

Connected rooms also require attention to direction, edges, and transitions. Planning those details together helps the new floor continue through the intended spaces with a coordinated appearance and appropriate connections to surfaces that will remain.

Material Direction

Hardwood or Engineered Hardwood?

Hardwood and engineered hardwood are both available installation directions for residential flooring projects. The appropriate discussion depends on the existing conditions, intended appearance, room layout, and requirements of the proposed work.

Plank dimensions, colour, texture, and finish can be reviewed alongside the rooms and adjoining surfaces. Material selection is handled as part of the installation plan for the home.

Engineered-Hardwood Installation

Engineered Hardwood Flooring in Mississauga

Engineered-hardwood flooring can be planned for individual rooms or across several connected areas. The installation scope can address existing-floor removal, applicable preparation, plank layout, transitions, baseboards, trims, and other finishing details.

A completed project in Lorne Park provides a local example of this work. Its specifications and installation details are described below and remain specific to that home.

Mississauga Hardwood Project

Lorne Park Engineered Hardwood Project

Loyalty Contracting completed an engineered-hardwood flooring modernization across approximately 1,200 sq. ft. of the main floor in a large detached home in Lorne Park, Mississauga.

01

Rooms Completed

The flooring scope covered the living room, dining room, family room, main hallway, and home office.

02

Initial Mixed Flooring

Before the work, the living and dining rooms had older, narrow reddish-brown strip hardwood. The family room and home office had faded, worn carpet.

03

European White Oak

The selected premium engineered hardwood was European White Oak in a 7.5-inch wide-plank format.

04

Project Finish

The new flooring had a wire-brushed, matte finish in a light natural sand tone.

05

Installation Method

For this project, the engineered hardwood was installed using a combined glue-and-cleat approach.

Project-Specific Preparation

Preparation and Subfloor Work on the Lorne Park Project

The preparation described here responds to the conditions found in this home and is not presented as a requirement for every hardwood installation.

01

Old-Floor Removal

The crew removed the old hardwood and carpet, along with the carpet underpad, old staples, and tack strips.

02

OSB Surface Cleaning

The OSB subfloor was scraped clean of old adhesive and debris before the next stages of work.

03

Response to Existing Squeaks

The existing subfloor was screwed down in response to squeaks found during this Lorne Park project.

04

Localized Low Area

Self-leveling compound was used in a low area near the family room to create a more level installation surface.

Project-Specific Finishing

Finishing Details on the Lorne Park Project

Baseboards, door openings, transitions, and the existing main staircase were coordinated with the new light-oak flooring on this project.

01

New Baseboards

New 7-inch modern square-edge MDF baseboards were installed, caulked, and painted bright white.

02

Door Jambs and Casings

The door jambs and casings were undercut so the flooring could continue beneath them with a clean finished appearance.

03

Oak Transitions

Custom flush-mount oak transition strips were made where the engineered hardwood meets the existing kitchen tile.

04

Existing Main Staircase

The staircase treads and handrail were sanded and refinished. A custom stain blend was used to coordinate them with the new light-oak flooring on this project.

Lorne Park Project Logistics

Project-Site Coordination

Heavy-duty dust barriers using ZipWall systems were installed to help separate the work area from the kitchen and second floor during this Lorne Park project.

Heavy living-room furniture was shifted in stages. This allowed the flooring installation to continue without requiring the homeowners to rent a storage pod. These arrangements describe this project; site logistics are planned around each home's conditions and scope.

FAQ

Mississauga Hardwood Flooring Questions

Useful information for homeowners preparing to discuss a residential flooring project.

Do you install hardwood flooring in Mississauga?

Yes. Hardwood floor installation can be planned for individual rooms, connected living areas, or a wider residential flooring project in Mississauga.

Do you install engineered hardwood?

Yes. Loyalty Contracting installs engineered-hardwood flooring. The material direction, rooms, installation approach, and finishing scope are reviewed for the individual project.

Can old flooring be removed before installation?

Yes. Old-floor removal can be included in the agreed scope before the new flooring is installed. The removal and preparation required depend on the existing materials and conditions.

Can hardwood be installed across multiple connected rooms?

Yes. Flooring can be coordinated across several connected rooms, with layout direction, transitions, edges, and adjoining surfaces considered during planning.

How is the subfloor assessed before installation?

The existing floor is reviewed for conditions relevant to the proposed installation. Preparation needs vary by property. On the Lorne Park project, the subfloor was screwed down in response to existing squeaks, and self-leveling compound was used in one low area.

Can transitions and baseboards be coordinated with the new flooring?

Yes. Transitions, baseboards, trims, and edges can be included where they form part of the agreed installation and finishing scope.

Do you have a completed engineered-hardwood project in Mississauga?

Yes. The Lorne Park project described on this page covered approximately 1,200 sq. ft. of the main floor in a large detached home and used 7.5-inch European White Oak engineered-hardwood planks.
