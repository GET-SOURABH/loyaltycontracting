Planning the Project

Hardwood Flooring Planning in Brampton

Flooring planning starts with the rooms involved, the condition of the existing surfaces, and the material direction selected for the home. The proposed scope can then account for removal, preparation, installation layout, and adjoining floors.

Transitions, baseboards, door trim, and applicable stair details should be considered with the floor rather than left as disconnected decisions. The exact work depends on the existing home and the agreed installation scope.

Supported Flooring Option

Engineered Hardwood Flooring

Engineered hardwood is one supported option for residential hardwood flooring projects. Product selection, installation method, plank direction, preparation, and finishing are reviewed in relation to the proposed rooms and existing surfaces.

The Peel Village project below is a local example using wide-plank European White Oak engineered hardwood. Its specifications and installation details apply to that home.

Brampton Hardwood Project

Peel Village Main-Floor Hardwood Project

Loyalty Contracting completed approximately 1,150 sq. ft. of engineered hardwood flooring across the main level of a detached 1970s home in Peel Village, Brampton. The kitchen was excluded from this flooring scope.

01

Existing Main-Floor Surfaces

The main floor had a mix of original 2 1/4-inch narrow-strip oak hardwood and dated 12x12 ceramic tile through the front foyer and main hallway.

02

Existing Hardwood Condition

The original hardwood had been refinished multiple times over its life and, based on its condition, was not considered suitable for another refinishing.

03

European White Oak

The new floor was premium European White Oak engineered hardwood in a 6.5-inch wide-plank format.

04

Warm Natural Finish

The planks had a warm honey, light natural wood tone with a matte wire-brushed finish.

05

Project Installation Method

The engineered hardwood was installed using a glue-assist and cleat-fastened method, combining polyurethane construction adhesive with mechanical fastening on this project.

Peel Village Preparation

Floor Removal and Subfloor Preparation

The following removal and preparation details responded to the conditions found in this Peel Village home. The preparation needed depends on the condition of the existing floor and subfloor.

01

Old-Floor Removal

The original strip hardwood, old flooring staples, ceramic tile, and wire mesh beneath the tile were removed, taking the work areas back to the exposed plywood subfloor.

02

Existing Plywood Subfloor

The original plywood subfloor was structurally sound overall, with some unevenness, notable deflection near the former hallway transition, and many existing squeaks.

03

Movement and Squeak Work

More than 400 specialized subfloor screws were installed into the joists to address existing movement and squeaks on this project.

04

Former Tiled Hallway

Cement-based self-leveling compound was used in the former tiled hallway to bring the surface into alignment with the adjoining living-room subfloor.

05

Front-Entry Plywood Repair

A damaged plywood section measuring approximately 2 by 3 feet near the front entry was removed and replaced before installation. The damage was associated with repeated moisture exposure from wet winter footwear.

Peel Village Finish Details

Transitions, Baseboards and Detail Work

The transition, trim, and limited stair work described here formed part of this specific main-floor installation.

01

White Oak Transition

A custom flush White Oak transition was fabricated between the new hardwood and the existing kitchen porcelain tile.

02

New Baseboards

The old 3-inch clamshell baseboards were removed and replaced after removal damage. New 5.5-inch square-edge MDF baseboards were installed, caulked, and painted bright white.

03

Door Jambs and Casings

Door jambs and casings were carefully undercut with a jamb saw so the new flooring could run beneath the existing trim.

04

First Three Stair Steps

The entire staircase was not replaced. Carpet was removed from only the first three steps, where custom White Oak retro-treads were installed and the risers were painted white to coordinate with the main-floor hardwood.

Project-Specific Layout

Planning the Plank Layout

Because some exterior walls in the Peel Village home were not perfectly square, the installation layout was planned around the longest continuous sightline from the front toward the rear of the main floor.

The chosen direction established the visual alignment through the connected areas, with custom angled perimeter cuts made where required by the walls in this particular home.

Peel Village Project Logistics

Protecting the Occupied Home During Flooring Work

The homeowners and their dog remained in the house during the project. Heavy-duty ZipWall plastic barriers separated the active flooring areas from the kitchen and the staircase leading to the second floor.

Heavy furniture was shifted in two phases, so no storage pod was required for this job. Completed areas were covered with Ram Board as the installation progressed. At the end of workdays on this project, the active areas were swept and cleaned with a HEPA-filter vacuum.

FAQ

Brampton Hardwood Flooring Questions

Information for homeowners preparing to discuss a residential hardwood installation.

Do you install hardwood flooring in Brampton?

Yes. Loyalty Contracting provides residential hardwood installation in Brampton, including room-specific, connected-space, and broader main-floor projects.

Do you install engineered hardwood flooring?

Yes. Engineered hardwood is a supported installation option. The material, proposed rooms, existing surface, and installation scope are reviewed for each project.

Can existing flooring be removed before installation?

Yes. Existing-floor removal can be included in the agreed scope. The materials and conditions are reviewed before the necessary removal and preparation work is defined.

What happens if the subfloor needs preparation?

Preparation depends on the conditions found after assessment or removal. On the Peel Village project, specific work addressed existing movement, unevenness, and a small damaged plywood area. The exact preparation depends on the existing flooring and subfloor.

Can new hardwood meet existing kitchen tile?

Transitions to adjoining surfaces can be planned as part of the installation. For the Peel Village home, a custom flush White Oak transition connected the new hardwood to the kitchen's existing porcelain tile.

Are baseboards and trim part of a flooring project?

Baseboards, casings, transitions, and related trim work can be included when they form part of the agreed flooring and finishing scope.

Can hardwood be installed while homeowners remain in the house?

Project logistics depend on the home and scope. The Peel Village homeowners remained in their house while barriers, phased furniture movement, surface protection, and project-specific cleanup measures were used during the work.
