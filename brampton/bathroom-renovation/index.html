Planning the Room

Bathroom Renovation Planning in Brampton

Planning starts with the bathroom's existing condition, available layout, storage, and the way the shower or bathtub is used. Lighting, flooring, ventilation, and finish priorities can then be reviewed in relation to the work being considered.

A defined scope helps organize demolition, preparation, material selections, installation, and final detailing. The appropriate combination depends on the individual room and the homeowner's priorities.

Everyday Function

Planning a Functional Family Bathroom

A family bathroom plan can balance storage, shower or bathtub practicality, useful lighting, and durable finish selections. Where the room dimensions and project scope allow, a double vanity can also support shared everyday use.

These choices should be considered together so that the room works for the household without assuming that the same layout suits every home.

Brampton Bathroom Project

Sandringham-Wellington Family Bathroom Project

Loyalty Contracting completed a main family bathroom renovation in a detached two-storey home in Sandringham-Wellington, Brampton.

01

Initial Bathroom

The early-2000s builder-grade bathroom had peeling sheet-vinyl flooring, a yellowing acrylic tub surround, signs of mold in the existing caulking, a single-sink vanity with limited family storage, and a loud, ineffective exhaust fan.

02

Bathtub and Tile

The old one-piece acrylic tub unit was removed and replaced with a deep acrylic alcove soaker tub. Glossy white 3x12-inch ceramic subway tile was installed to the ceiling in a vertical stack pattern.

03

Recessed Niche

The tiled surround included a recessed niche finished with grey hexagonal accent tile.

04

Vanity and Storage

A 60-inch double vanity with a navy-blue finish and white quartz countertop was installed with two recessed mirrored medicine cabinets. The original plumbing footprint was maintained while the double-sink setup improved family storage and use.

05

Fixtures

Brushed-nickel fixtures included a dual-showerhead system with a fixed rainfall-style shower head and an adjustable handheld wand.

06

Lighting and Flooring

Three damp-rated recessed LED pot lights and LED vanity sconces above the mirrors were installed. The floor was finished with 12x24-inch matte grey porcelain tile in a brick-lay pattern.

Sandringham-Wellington Project Details

Waterproofing, Ventilation and Utility Work on the Project

The following details describe this specific family bathroom project. The exact work depends on the existing bathroom and the scope of the renovation.

01

Waterproofing and Moisture-Resistant Work

A Schluter-KERDI waterproofing membrane system was applied to the tub walls before tile installation, and moisture-resistant green-board drywall was installed in the room.

02

Ventilation

The existing exhaust fan was replaced with a 110 CFM Panasonic WhisperQuiet unit, and the exhaust was rerouted to vent through the roof rather than the soffit.

03

Plumbing

The original plumbing footprint was maintained. Aging supply lines were replaced with PEX, and a new tub drain and overflow assembly was installed.

04

Electrical Work

The project included a new 20-amp GFCI-protected vanity outlet, exhaust-fan wiring, and main bathroom lighting wiring. The fan and main lighting were placed on separate switches.

Project-Specific Electrical Coordination

ESA Coordination on the Sandringham-Wellington Project

For this project, an ESA electrical authorization was secured for the new GFCI outlet, vanity lighting, and exhaust-fan work. A final electrical inspection was completed, and an ESA certificate was provided to the homeowners.

These details apply to the Sandringham-Wellington project. Electrical and permit requirements depend on the property and proposed work.

Occupied-Home Project

Protecting the Home During the Renovation

This project involved the main second-floor bathroom in an occupied family home. Heavy-duty neoprene runners were installed from the front door, up the stairs, and along the hallway to protect the existing flooring during demolition and tile work.

The work areas were vacuumed and packed up at the end of each workday on this project.

FAQ

Brampton Bathroom Renovation Questions

Information for Brampton homeowners preparing to discuss a bathroom project.

What bathroom renovation services do you provide in Brampton?

Depending on the agreed scope, bathroom work can include layout updates, showers or bathtubs, tile, vanities, mirrors, storage, fixtures, lighting, flooring, waterproof finishes, painting, trims, demolition, preparation, and final detailing.

Can a bathtub and tile surround be replaced together?

Bathtub and tile-surround work can be coordinated as part of the same agreed scope. On the Sandringham-Wellington project, the old tub unit was replaced with an acrylic alcove soaker tub, and ceramic tile was installed to the ceiling around it.

Can vanity storage be improved during a bathroom renovation?

Vanity, medicine-cabinet, shelving, and storage options can be reviewed in relation to the available room. The Sandringham-Wellington project included a double vanity and two recessed mirrored medicine cabinets.

How are flooring and finish selections planned?

Flooring, tile, fixtures, paint, trims, hardware, and other visible details can be reviewed together so the selections align with the approved bathroom scope.

Can ventilation, plumbing or electrical work be coordinated when part of the project?

Applicable ventilation, plumbing, and electrical work can be discussed after the existing bathroom and proposed scope are reviewed. Requirements vary by project.

How do you protect occupied areas of the home during renovation?

Protection measures depend on the property and work area. For the Sandringham-Wellington project, neoprene runners protected the route from the front door to the second-floor bathroom, and work areas were vacuumed and packed up at the end of each workday.

Can permit or electrical requirements be discussed when applicable?

Yes. Requirements depend on the property and proposed work. On the Sandringham-Wellington project, an ESA electrical authorization was secured for the GFCI outlet, vanity lighting, and exhaust-fan work; a final inspection was completed and a certificate was provided.
